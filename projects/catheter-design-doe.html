Catheter Design & DOE

From high-resolution imaging to CFD-based design exploration of paediatric haemodialysis catheters
Micro-CT · CAD · CFD · DOE · Python

The Challenge

Central venous catheters are widely used for paediatric haemodialysis, but their design can lead to unfavourable flow conditions, including stagnation, unbalanced flow distribution and high shear stresses.

This case study combines computational characterisation of existing catheter designs with a systematic exploration of the geometric parameters that influence haemodynamic performance.

I was Principal Investigator of an NIHR-funded project developing a data-driven approach to investigate current limitations of central venous lines for haemodialysis in children and support the development of novel design solutions.

Workflow

01 · Micro-CT Imaging and geometric assessment
02 · CAD Geometry reconstruction and parametrisation
03 · CFD Haemodynamic characterisation
04 · DOE Systematic design-space exploration

Geometry Reconstruction

High-resolution micro-CT images were used to reconstruct accurate three-dimensional catheter geometries, including the arterial and venous lumens, tips and side holes.

Geometric measurements were extracted from the reconstructed models and used to generate controlled computational geometries for the subsequent CAD and CFD analyses.

CAD Design

The measured geometries were translated into CAD models to create controlled and reproducible computational geometries.

The catheter features were subsequently parametrised to enable the systematic variation of geometric characteristics such as side-hole diameter and position and tip geometry.

CFD Characterisation

The reconstructed catheter geometries were analysed using computational fluid dynamics to investigate flow behaviour under paediatric haemodialysis conditions.

Blood flow distribution, velocity, residence time, shear stress and platelet lysis index were evaluated to compare different catheter designs and identify haemodynamic criticalities.

The analysis showed low-flow regions near the arterial tip and an unbalanced distribution of flow between the tip and side holes. Anatomical models also showed increased flow disturbance and wall shear stress following catheter placement.

From CFD to Parametrisation

Building on the CFD characterisation, the geometry of two catheter models was parametrised to investigate which design features had the strongest influence on haemodynamic performance.

The parametrisation included side-hole diameter and position together with geometric parameters describing the catheter tip. The Tesio 6.5F model included 21 design variables, while the Hemo-Cath 8F model included 9 variables.

Design of Experiments

A Design of Experiments framework was developed to systematically explore the resulting design space and investigate the influence of individual geometric parameters and their interactions.

Latin Hypercube Sampling was used to generate the design points. The sampled geometries were automatically transferred through the CAD and CFD workflow, while Python scripts were used to filter invalid geometries, label the side holes and post-process the computational results.

The analysis considered blood flow rate, shear stress, residence time and platelet lysis index as performance indicators.

What the Analysis Showed

The DOE identified the proximal side holes as some of the most influential geometric features, affecting both flow rates and shear stress.

The influence of individual features depended on the catheter lumen and operating flow rate, highlighting the importance of considering the interaction between catheter geometry and haemodynamic conditions.

The findings were translated into practical design recommendations concerning the number, position and diameter of side holes, as well as the diameter and positioning of the tip opening.

The computational workflow enabled systematic comparison of catheter geometries and provided a basis for future paediatric-specific catheter development.
